# Learner's Mindset
In my opinion the best way to achieve having a learner's mindset is to avoid falling into the trap of the Dunning Kruger Effect. For those unaware of this effect, basically it is a function of how smart you think you are in a subject vs how smart you actually are. The general idea is that there are people who know nothing on a subject and are aware of this fact. However, when people start to learn a little bit about a topic they tend to believe they know a lot more than they actually do. If they choose to learn more in said topic then they start to understand exactly how much they do not know. 

In my opinion, coding is an excellent example of this. There are many people that steer away from coding because they know they have no idea how to code. Then there are the coding beginners who think they know alot more about coding than they actually do, take for example a guy who doesn't have much coding experience making an app by himself and leaking a bunch of keys and exposing vulnerabilities. As people learn more about computer science they start to understand that theres a wide variety of fields needed to be learned such as DSA and theories.

To avoid falling into this track one should always have a beginners mindset, where they think they do not know much and constantly ask questions to learn more. Of course they should be able to answer questions when asked but in order to learn you need to ask questions.
