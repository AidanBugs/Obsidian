# Goal Setting
People have things that want to achieve, ambitions to be realized, etc. These things are often made into goals, regardless of whether a person is aware this is truly a goal of theirs. However, there are a variety of ways to help people actually achieve their goals.

## Planning
Planning is the most important part of actually attaining your goals. In order to achieve one's goals, it is best for them to plan how they will actually achieve them.

### Action Steps
First create action steps. Step away from an abstract goal of "get in shape", "do better in school", etc and focus on more concrete action steps to achieve these goals. I think having the overarching goal is fine, but really breaking goals down into small action steps is what makes them possible. Think about if you were assigned a research paper, this can seem like a daunting task and thus you might push it off for later. Instead, if the task was broken up into, "what do you want to research?", "what data would you need?", "where can you get that data?", "how would you analyze it?", all of a sudden this research paper is much easier to write. 

Taking this concept into our goals of "get in shape" or "do better in school", we need to break them into action steps. For the former, our action steps could be start exercising more or eat healthier, which is a great first start but we can improve this even more in the following sections.

### Be Specific
Being specific gets rid of any uncertainty or anxiety stemming from not knowing what / how to do a daunting task which is why this planning phase is so important. In the case of our exercise goal, we can say "after school / work I will go to the gym". Okay good start but we could be more specific by removing potential distractions such as going home first, so now "after school / work I will go straight to the gym". We can be more specific by starting to specify which gym, what kind of workout, how long, etc. All of this helps take away any confusion we may have.

### Start Small
Don't expect you to miraculously be able to run a 5 minute mile, run for an hour a day, etc etc. Understanding where you at currently in the goal and maintaining a reasonable pace prevents burnout which ultimately leads to not achieving your goals.

### Define Obstacles
Think of these like edge cases in programming, your goal and action steps will work most of the time but what about for uncommon situations? For example, what if the gym is closed? or if you need to work overtime? or there is a deadline you have to meet one night? All of these might prevent you from going to the gym, but planning ahead of time for these cases is what determines who can really suceed. This could be as simple as "I'll workout for half the time", or even making it a non-negotiable to just workout tomorrow also works too!

### Systems beat Motivation
This whole planning step is just to reiterate the idea that systems beat motivation, especially with a well laid out meticulous system. Systems can even plan for times when theres no motivation. "If I'm not motivated that morning I will do one pushup" and now boom you exercised, which is better than having not have exercised and more often than not that one pushup turns into a few more.

## Acting on your Plan
You have a full action oriented system to help achieve your goals, all thats left is to take action. It is important that your system is not some impossibly difficult challenge (unless its for a short duration and for funsies) but something that is easy to stay committed to. Work towards your goals one at a time, take small steps (tortise beats the rabbit), be flexible for obstacles or lack of motivation. Good luck.
