I think the article does an excellent job of explaining the very explicit differences between the two paradigms of artificial intellegince in our society. On the one hand, algorithms (better known as hard AI or deterministic machines) are a good representation on how we idealize our own society. On the other hand, machine learning on labelled data (soft AI that are often nondeterministic) are a representation of how our society is currently operating. For the algorithms, it is much easier to readjust the algorithms as norms change. On the other hand, soft AI, which determines its own rules to label the data, peprpetuates the status quo and it is harder to change these sorts of algorithms as they rely on large amounts of existing data. As such, for soft AI even if norms change it is harder to use these sorts of systems as they will attempt to proceed with their former concieved notions of whatever they were trained on. Interestingly, last semester I did a project on algorithmic bias in AI resume screeners and found that smaller parameter language models tend to analyze and account for percieved race and gender more than the larger parameter models which tended to disregard these features. I think another issue people have with soft AI models is that they are black box systems which are not transparent with how the final decisions are made. I guess my question for yall is what domains are you comfortable with different levels of AIhaving the final say in decision making? For example, we already let hard AI determine our traffic lights, directions we take (google maps), and in some cases what content we are nudged to consume (music, news, videos, media in general although some algorithms here fall more under the soft AI). On the other hand, there are pushes towards soft AI in the court of law with facial recognition, self driving cars, and even more autonomous things like human robots.
