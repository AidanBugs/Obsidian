The Constanza-Chock reading presented a very clear analysis of how artifacts have politics through a personal anecdote of the author's TSA experiences. For many of us, the TSA check is hardly more than an inconvenience where (at worst) a TSA agent goes through our suitcase if it gets flagged. For Constanza-Chock, they talked about how the TSA checkpoint is a constant source of stress and anxiety as the body scan algorithm will almost always flag them as suspicious prompting a rather extensive patdown which causes discomfort among both the author and the TSA agent. Constanza-Chock used this as a perfect demonstration of how these machines promote CIS norms.

Constanza-Chock also brought up the Design Justice Network principles which are basically ways to ensure that the designs of artifacts are sustainable and empowering to all members of a community. While I applaud the creation of this Design Justice Network, I am skeptical about the actual impact it has. For example, it was described in Wiener's "Do Artifacts Have Politics" that the bridges designed by Robert Moses were designed to make it harder for lower income families that rely on public transportation to go to Robert Moses' state park. These bridges are still around today and although I admit is quite hard to fix now that they are materialized in the world but that is part of my point here. Sure we can enact Design Justice on things being created today, but we are often unable to alter those made in the past such as the US very dependent car economy while many other countries have very reliable public transport. Additionally, who is to say that if we have a fully just design now, there could be social norms in the future that would dissaprove of the current design choices. As a rather extreme example, imagine a world where we as a society (not just as individuals) no longer believe in eating animal meat. Countless farms and companies were designed to produce animal meat as consumer food and would likely fight to stop such a change.

Another important critique I have about the Design Justice Network is for artifacts that are designed with an inherent political property to them (better described in Wiener's reading). According to Wiener, these sorts of artifacts have politics embedded in their existence rather than their design. In these cases, no amount of Design Justice could remove the politics from these artifacts. So I guess my questions are how can we promote a flebile design principle to allow for the designs of artifacts to change if new social norms arise and how should we adress inherently political technologies (if one wishes to believe they exist)?
