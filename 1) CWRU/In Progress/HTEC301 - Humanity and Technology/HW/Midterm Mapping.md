# Research Statement
My research project project looks at questions of the purposes and differences between AI models and their meta datas. Specifically, analyzing model performances across AI benchmarks, which are used to measure the skills and capabilities of AI models in specific domains. For example, perhaps the most famous benchmark for AI is the "Humanity's Last Exam" which is a set of 2500 questions across a wide variety of disciplines curated by almost 1000 experts in their respective fields meant to represent the breadth and depth of human knowledge at the time. These questions were designed to be extremely difficult challenging model's breadth and depth of knowledge and even including multiple types of question formats some having text, images, and audio. The goal of this research is to perform a deep dive into which areas LLM's perform the best or are most lacking in. However this research aims to go much beyond simply surface level analysis of benchmarks. This project aims to look at the clustering/convergence of AI models across specialized domains and attempt to draw connections between culture and domain performance. As a rather naive and simple example, perhaps its found that models that perform well in coding benchmarks also perform well in higher level math bench marks creating a separate cluster from models performing well at creative tasks. To draw in the humanities to this research project, it is now important to look at culture and diversity among AI models, ie are AI models from China specializing in coding benchmarks suggesting a cultural focus on technological development and innovation. On top of the origin of models, I also want to add a temporal aspect to the analysis looking at improvement of performance over time but also shifts in category performances suggesting shifts in focus towards different domains as models release. As for how this project views technology, perhaps the view is technology as a medium for or mirror of the culture of its origin/society. Perhaps a potentially scary or dangerous view of technology and specifically AI is a view of it as a fully autonomous system and society in the sense that certain models are specialized in specific domains and that over time models are generally improving across all benchmarks. Think of how in human and animal "societies" alike, certain groups are selected for certain tasks and it being the collection of these groups and the teamwork of these groups working together which brings about the most success of the societies. As a hypothesis for a temporal aspect of this research, perhaps the beginning improvements of AI were more general in the sense that we hadn't focused on creating specialized models and were striving for an AGI as opposed to a collection of specialized models approaching an AGI. This project is deeply rooted in ideas of social constructivism or simply that society shapes how technology manifests itself and improves in our world. Specifically trying to understand and analyze models compared to thier origin to ask questions on if certain regions valued improvements in different disciplines more than others. However this paper will also consequentially study the pairwase association / covariances between different disciplines and categories perhaps expaning on ways in which disciplines are connected and clustered.

Tam, Adrian. "Principal Component Analysis for Visualization." MachineLearningMastery.com, October 20, 2021. https://machinelearningmastery.com/principal-component-analysis-for-visualization/.

> Although I have not fully decided how I want to represent the benchmark data, I am certain that I will use principal component analysis to visualize the high dimensionality of the data in a simpler 2D or 3D space. For my data, one row of the dataset would correspond to a single model, and contain the model's name, its meta data (origin and release date) as well as the performance of the model either by specific benchmarks or by specific benchmark categories (ie domain knowledge). An abstracted definition of principal components analysis is that a single component would represent multiple columns of the original data set in a way that explains the most amount of variance within the original data. Essentially, PCA allows for us to project high dimensionality data into much smaller dimensions while preserving the meaning of all the data. This allows for significantly better visualizations for humans to understand and interpret because a 2 or 3 dimensional graph is significantly easier to read and interpret than attempting to visualize say 5D or higher (X,Y,Z, color of a point, size of a point, shape of a point is a "typical" way of attempting to model high dimensional data but allways gets confusing).

Akhtar, Muneeb, Ameen Ali, Siva Reddy, and Dzmitry Bahdanau. 2026. "When AI Benchmarks Plateau: A Systematic Study of Benchmark Saturation." arXiv preprint arXiv:2602.12345.

> This very recent paper was released in febuary of 2026 and analyzes benchmark "saturation" which refers to how strong a benchmark is over time. In other words, does the benchmark have many models reaching a max score after only a few months? The paper found that public vs private benchmarks did not have a significant effect on reducing model saturation, rather benchmarks curated by experts were more resistant to saturation than crowd sourced ones. This paper made me realize I should also indicate / analyze / consider how the benchmarks were made when it comes to determining a model's performance within a domain. The paper covered a wide variety of benchmark design's effect on saturation such as public/private testing sets, english only benchmarks, presence of synthetic benchmark questions, response type (true/false & multiple choice vs open ended), and even benchmark usage. This paper in a way acts as the CS foundation for my paper as it directly attacks different design choices of benchmarks and analyzes AI performance over time on said benchmarks.

Frontiers in Psychology. 2025. "Whose Bias Gets Coded? Psychology's Role in Decolonizing AI." Special issue, Frontiers in Psychology. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12621103/

> This paper analyzes a variety of different AI case studies, including facial recognition algorithms, bias in resume screening (I also did a paper on this and found that newer models were fairly robust against significant bias in resume screening as long as they had higher parameters (>10b)), image generative AI, predictive poolicing and more. The paper then describes different ways to combat bias in AI systems (defining bad bias as ones that enforce stereotypes and opression) is to have increased diversity in training data, particularly for local use cases. An example being that a health bot was commonly used in a zulu community but was trained on mostly english data so many points were lost in translation leading to incorrect diagnosis. While my research will not directly address the biases found in these case studies, it may find bias in terms of which categories of benchmarks are models making the most improvements in, suggesting a focus of which sorts of problems are seen as most important by AI researching companies or by culture (ie western vs eastern).

Ramezani, Aida, and Yang Xu. 2025. "The Discordance Between Embedded Ethics and Cultural Inference in Large Language Models." Preprint, submitted January 15, 2025. https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.743/

> This paper does a really great job of summarizing AI and culture. Specifically bringing up ideas of a LLM ethics hyperplane which acts as a differentiater between the wrong and right but tends to assert the dominant cultures beliefs onto the hyperplane. The example from the text is that when asking about polish culture and meeting people, the LLM would incorrectly assert that people maintain eye contact when that is infact not the cultural norm of polish people. Thus, suggesting lots of subtle cultural norms embedded within these LLM systems. This has potentially really interesting implications within my own research that I may not be able to find significant AI clustering performance by domain if I do not filter by cultural origin because benchmark performance may be mediated by cultural origin. Additionally, since many models are primarily trained on english texts, the potential "convergence" of AI might lead toward a homogenization effect of western culture as the US is the primary origin of many AI models.
