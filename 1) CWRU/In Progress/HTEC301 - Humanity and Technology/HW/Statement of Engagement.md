# 1. 
What does meaningful engagement look like for you in a seminar course? This might involve active participation in class discussion, thoughtful engagement with your peers' questions on Canvas and IRL, building and sharing resource guides on are.na or other platforms, coming to my office hours,. and more besides. 

> I think the short few suggestions after the question can demonstrate at a very broad surface level what engagement in the class might look like. However, I think there are a few more steps to make this form of engagement become meaningful engagement. For me, active participation should go beyond the frequency at which one contributes to the discussion, but rather learning to synthesize 3 main things. One's participation in the discussion should be a culmination of what is said or ideas from the text, what others have already said on the topic, as well as one's own opinions and experiences. Those 3 things are what really separates a person speaking up in class for the sake of speaking or for their grade, from someone who is thinking critically and meaningfully engaging with the discussion at hand. Simarly commenting on a peer's Canvas responses and questions should look very similar. One should engage with what the peer said on Canvas, and address topics and ideas from the text as well as incorporate their own opinions and experiences on the matter.

# 3. 
How would you define your own "progress" in the course? This might include both reachable and aspirational goals ie., you would like to improve your confidence participating in group discussions; you'd like to improve critical reading or writing skills; or, maybe there is a particular project you envision but need to develop certain creative or difficult technical skills. This is a wide-open question.

> For me, I think the biggest thing I want to improve on is formulating my own opinions and understanding of the topics covered in the texts. By no means am I expecting myself to become an expert in all topics covered in the texts, I simply wish to improve my capabilities of forming my own opinions of the theses covered in the text. The reason is because in middle school and high school, I often found myself agreeing with the arguements in the texts used in classes or simply agreeing with what ever the teacher's opinion on the text was. I believe this may have formed from either an internal desire to ofload the mental energy used to actually think critically about the text/arguement, or from a trained pattern of agreeing with the text/teacher resulted in a higher grade on the assignment. Regardless, I want to continue enhancing my ability to form logicly sound arguements (for or against the text) beyond simply agreeing or piggy backing what others have already laid out before me. On top of this, I always challenge myself to be proud of the work I submit. In my comp sci courses, this often takes form in me having final projects that I thought were genuinely challenging or impactful (typically beyond the scope of the class). For example, in my introduction to data science class, me and my friend made a gerrymandering simulator to determine if states were likely under the influence of gerrymandering (or nonrandom creation) which was a project a thoroughly enjoyed and had a very fun impact beyond just a simple project for the sake of doing a class project.
