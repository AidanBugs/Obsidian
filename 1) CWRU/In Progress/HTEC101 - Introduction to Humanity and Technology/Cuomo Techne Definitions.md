# Moral Status of Techne
Occasionally techne was held in high regard whilst others they were looked down upon. One of which was the base technition which was looked down upon because Aristotle and Plato looked down on physical laborers as they believed these sorts of labors dulled the mind and the laborers would do anything for money. 

Additionally Aristotle in general held the user in higher regard than the maker (those in the domain of techne) stating that the maker was a subordinate of the user because the user has episteme of the object. The example given is the flute maker and the flautist, where the flautist is above the flute maker because they understand how to use the flute.

However in the example of medicine and doctors, whom do unpleasant things such as cut, burn and administer drugs all because they believe they know it will do good in the end. The arguement is however they know how to do these things to also do harm and thus leaving this practice simultaneously in a great usefulness but also moral ambiguity.
