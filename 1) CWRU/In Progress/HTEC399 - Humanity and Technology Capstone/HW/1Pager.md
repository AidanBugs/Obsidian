## Mapping the Cultural Fingerprints of LLM's
**Background**

AI, specifically LLM's, are a very big topic all over the world right now. People generally praise how flexible these models are and how they can do almost anything. There are dozens if not hundreds of different AI companies each with their own model(s) that are trying to sell to consumers that their AI model should be used over their competitors. Now besides public sentiment, how does one actually determine which AI model is better than the others? Thats where benchmarks come into play. Benchmarks are to LLM's as standardized tests are to students. Essentially people have created different benchmarks for the LLMs to determine how good a model is at a specific task (think specific AP tests to evaluate students capabilities on a subject). Now the typical use case of these benchmarks (by the public and by companies deciding which company to buy enterprise accounts for) typically analyze which models or AI company tends to be top performers at the benchmarks for their use cases. Noow typically people only look at how models perform on a specific benchmark or small set of benchmarks to determine which model is the best. But what if the constant creation of new models and benchmarks have underlying implications and features people have yet to notice?

**What is the Project**

My project studies AI benchmark performance in aggregate, rather than focusing only on top-performing models. I intervene by asking: what underlying factors shape what we call “performance”? While many people assume LLM benchmarks are assumed to be neutral leaderboards, I question this assumption by studying patterns that emerge from mapping LLM models onto a coordinate space, based on their benchmark performance, where models that perform similarly across benchmarks cluster together. I hope to find whether regional origin predicts performance patterns and whether domain strengths form distinct cognitive styles. If so, this changes the landscape: benchmarks become cultural artifacts rather than neutral yardsticks; AI evaluation becomes a site of cultural negotiation; and “intelligence” must be understood as plural, situated, and political.

**Significance**

Benchmarks are quietly making cultural policy since they determine which models get funded, deployed, and regulated. If a benchmark encodes one tradition's definition of "correct reasoning," then evaluation is a mechanism of cultural control. Additionally my project challenges the idea of the "AI Race" metaphor. If models from different origins develop distinct cognitive profiles (math+logic vs math+reasoning), then we are not watching a single ladder being climbed, rahter divergent intellectual traditions being compressed into one ranking. If every lab optimizes toward the same benchmark(s), diversity of problem-solving style erodes, whats worse is that erosion is labelled progress.
