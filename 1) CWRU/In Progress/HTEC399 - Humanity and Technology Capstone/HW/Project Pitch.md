I will be building a critical tool that reimagines how we compare AI systems. Instead of relying on a flat leaderboard, my project constructs a "cultural map" of large language models using their benchmark scores as a coordinate system. This technique, known as embeddings, places models that perform similarly on tests like reasoning or math close together, while fundamentally different models drift apart, revealing distinct "fingerprints" of knowledge. The urgency is that we are currently designing intelligence without understanding the cultural values embedded within it. As my initial findings show, a machine learning algorithm can predict whether a model is from the US or China with nearly 90% accuracy based purely on its test scores. This proves that performance is a reliable cultural fingerprint, not simply because models from one region are simply better at math or reasoning than another, but because the training data, the benchmark questions, and the very definition of "correct reasoning" are all co-constructed artifacts of specific epistemological traditions. The classifier exposes that these tests are lenses, not mirrors, of universal intelligence. This project matters for policymakers, developers, and the public, as it unveils the hidden cultural negotiations behind technological "progress." The two deliverables will be an improved, user-friendly interactive dashboard that empowers anyone to explore this map, and a research paper that formally documents the construction of this embedding space and its profound implications for understanding AI as a thoroughly social and political technology.
