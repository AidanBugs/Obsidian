# Angier Techne in the Platonic Dialogues
This section mainly focuses on Socrates and his views on Techne and if there exists a virtue techne. Techne is described as a skill stating that when looking to make a ship you ask a ship maker because they have the techne of ship making. The first couple pages are about whether or not a virtue techne exists and the benefits of one if it exists. His point is that the wisest of people are unable to pass on their virtues onto anyone else and that the wise ones who try to think of who taught them their virtues cannot think of a person (68). Thus somehow coming to the conclusion the only hope of sustaining the ethical well being of society across generation is a virtue techne, which seems counterintuitive to me. 

Techne has 5 key parts: professionalism, rationality, control, transmissability and agreement. To me this proves that no such virtue techne can exist because the agreeability of a set of virtue is subject to change from person to person based on their differences in value frameworks. In a rather rudimentary example, certain people value the preservation of self over others and would not sacrifice themself to save say 2 people for example, whilst others might make the sacrifice. This doesn't make a person unvirtuous simply because they have a higher value of themself. Both groups of people would have their own reasons justified by their own set of values causing them to disagree on the matter.

Additionally Techne is divided into Productive (tangible) and Theoretical, which are fairly straightforward categories. 

# Aristotle - Episteme and Techne
Aristotle defines scientific knowledge as Episteme, defining science as a very definitive thing that can be both taught and learned. 

He then defines craft knowledge as Techne, concerned only with the production of things and not actions. 

Intelligence (Phronesis) is defined as the ability to understand what to do to live well. Aristotle that Phronesis is different from Techne and Episteme because they vary in scope. Additionally Aristotle argues that Phronesis cannot be forgotten. 

Understanding (Nous) refers to the history and origin of things, which is distinct from the other groups which do not concern themselves with origin of things. 

Wisdom (Sophia) the combination of Episteme and Nous, in the sense that a wise person is very excellent in their field and must have a deep understanding of the Episteme and the Nous.
