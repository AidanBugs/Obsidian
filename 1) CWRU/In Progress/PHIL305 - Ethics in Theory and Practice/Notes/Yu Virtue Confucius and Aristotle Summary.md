"Virtue" is translated from the latin word "virtus" where "vir" translates as "manhood"

[^2]: Need for both parts (EX: Bank Robber): "fond of ren without being fond of learning is liable to lead to foolishness" "Learning means to learn li, to recognize it and embody it in one's behavior" (Pg 324)

**Ethos and Practical Wisdom**

"Li is thus the totality of socially acceptable behavior patterns and lifestyles, including both moral and non-moral norms. It corresponds to Aristotle's ethos (social custom), that is, the traditional social mores and cultural settings" (Pg 326)

The "return to li" for Confucius refers to returning to the lif of the Zhou dynasty. Confucius believes that the reason behind social turmoils of his time was the "loss of traditional values of the Zhou culture" (Pg 327). 

Aristotle believes in conformity to the prevailing social customs because of the social nature of humans and the difference between a "good man " and a "good citizen".

Be reflective toward *ethos* (social custom) through phronesis in order to be a good person. Phroensis is needed because:

1. Understand why behaviours are noble and true
2. Compare various views of what goodness is
3. Understands context / particulars (Universals vs Particulars)

According to Yu, "Confucius' notion of yi [righteousness] seems to open the door for an Aristotelian practical wisdom, but he fails to work it out. He has a major virtue called "wisdom" (zhi), but it must also be based on conformity to li" (Pg 331)


**Filial Love and Self-love**
"Filial piety and brotherly love are the roots of ren" (Pg 332) is centered around this idea that "a good father makes a good ruler, a good son makes a good subject" (Pg 332).

For Aristotle, love/friendship is "intrinsic to a happy life" (Pg 334). Unlike Confucius, Aristotle believes these relationships are all "an extension of one's love of oneself rather than an extension of filial love" (Pg 335)
