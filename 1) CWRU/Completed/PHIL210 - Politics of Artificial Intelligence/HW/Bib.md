# 1) Rhetorical description.
This paper will orient itself around the discussions within the realms of philosophy of technology, political philosophy, and those critical of technology's role in our societies. More specifically, engaging with critical debates about technologies role in shaping (and diminishing) human agency. This arguement aims to synthesize 20th century philosophies about the dehumanizing nature of technologies, most notably Deleuze's "dividual" and Mumford's "Megamachine" with 21st century problems and issues. Additionally, this paper will look at the supposedly new phenomenon that humans are being reduced to components within a larger system and dive into its roots as well as its unprecidented scale within the digital age. 

This paper aims to extend many of the historic notions such as Mumford's "Megamachine" to more 21st century "New Megamachine", which uses algorithms and monitoring to optimize human behaviour and create a more effecient (and even less human) Megamachine. Additionally, extending upon Deleuze's "dividual" and "societies of control" to real life implementations of these in the "Golden State Killer" investigation which demonstrated how humans are parsed as data points and can be used for extreme intrusive social management. 


# 2) Outlines of Sources 1-10.
## 1
This text is an overall critique on reductionism and Jones argues that breaking down complex entities into its components fails to capture the emergent reality of the whole.

## 2
This article argues that modern AI and various forms of data analytics is creating a new digital "Megamachine" where humans are reduced to standardized and monitored components in a system.

## 3
Technology is more than just a set of external tools but an integral part of human nature. However, human's are much more than just tool makers as they have many more things that differentiate humans from other primates than just the use of tools.

## 4
This article examines Wiener's approach of reducing enemy human pilots to a servomechanism within a plane. Causing the reduction of a human pilot to a black box with inputs and outputs.

## 5
Deleuze proposes a type of society called the "societies of control", which operate through continuous modulation, monitoring and access management rather than enclosed spaces. In these societies, the indivisible individual is replaced by the "dividual", a fragmented codeable data subject.

## 6
This is an encyclopedia entry that thoroughly outlines what is reductionism which in short is that any complex system is nothing more than the sum of its parts. 

## 7
This article is a case study of Deleuze's dividual, showing how a killer was found via DNA of their family members. The article shows how we are part of Deleuze's societies of control where individuals are parsed into data points (DNA) and subject to scary digital control.

## 8
Early example of the commodification of humans, where people are packaged and marketed as products. This article shows how people were packaged and marketed as products looking at various categories of the human that makes them marketable such as physical status, education, occupation, money, etc.

## 9
This is research on the economic effects and concerns of predictive genetic testing which frames individual's future health risks as predictable calculatable costs in a healthcare system.

## 10
A study that found that humans who use AI recommenders often percieve them as mindless things, which reduce the user's mentalizing process, which is the ability for humans to understand the mental state of others. In this case, looking at how the AI recommenders are equally as reliable as human recommenders, thus making the arguement that humans can be reduced to machines with inputs and outputs.
