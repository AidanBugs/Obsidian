# Reading Response Paper 2
The overarching claim of this paper is comparing and contrasting the ancient use and meaning of the word techne with contemporary notions of technology. Tabachnick aims to analyze these terms beyond simple quantitative and qualitative analysis and really wants to dive deep into the naunces between the terms. Specifically, analyzing the associated tragedies between the two terms and how people percieve this said association. 

The first section techne and technology is a nice overview of the two terms as well as other people's approaches to the comparisons of techne with technology. Some point out that technology refers to the product itself while techne is more knowledge about the physical product. However people also use the term technology to describe techinical knowledge of a craft (the text gave the example of metalworking), leading to some people believing the two terms are indead similar. On the other hand, Tabachnick references the Canadian philosopher George Grant's idea that the ancient term techne was very limited in what was and was not considered a techne whereas the contemporary term technology is differentiated by the lack of any limit. 

Next, Tabachnick recites Thucydides, arguing that there is an inevitable tragedy associated with the advancements in techne and crafts. With advancements in pyrotechnics, the Peloponnesians used fire as a weapon in war only for them to be countered by natures rain. With advancements in the interconnected port systems of the Athenians, they were met with a plague that spread faster than any disease they've ever dealt with. Tabachnick uses these examples to show that there is an inevitable tragedy with greater power and that the rapid increases in the skills of the Athenians is what ironically lead to the collapse of their empire. Tabachnick then recalls the various plays and tragedies written by Sophocles, which are in general oriented around the inevitable tragedy that comes from human's greedy pursuit of knowledge or power.

Tabachnick then incorporates Heidigger's idea that the pre-Platonic notions of techne encompass an awareness of the tragedies associated with technology whereas contemporary technology denies such association and thus diminishes the awareness. Heidigger brings up the idea that techne works within the bounds of nature whereas technology seeks to challenge these bounds. The example given is that ancient craftsmen would work together with nature and use wood to make this chair, despite knowing that there is an inevitable decay in the wood over time. Whereas contemporary technology could seek to use genetic engineering to overcome this weakness or impurity of nature. Heidigger argues that there is a reflection within techne as it seeks to work within the bounds of nature rather than defy it.

Heidigger argues that there is a rather burdensome nature in contemporary technology because it lacks the beautiful temporary nature of techne. Additionally, despite the permanent imposition of technology, Heidigger argues we can always return to techne as the "beginning exists" and that it "stands before us". Heidigger argues that we should not seek to remove the tragic nature of techne through technology, rather embrace the tragic temporary nature of techhne. Heidigger goes on to argue that technology is stultifying and that tragedy is the exact remedy to this stultifying force. 

Tabachnick then questions if Heidigger is right and if we should go back to techne and steer away from technology. Ultimately, Tabachnick reaches the conclusion that while technology aims to save humans from inhumane deaths and tragedies, technology in turn dehumanizes us by bringing us away from the very nature that makes us human. Tabachnick admits that while there is a seemingly limitless world behind technology, we as humans should still proceed with caution if we wish to keep our humanity.

Overall, Tabachnick's paper follows a general structure of first introducing the overarching theme of the paper then allowing the reader to understand the terms used within the paper and bringing in insight from other people's works. He then analyzes the works of the ancient Athenians and Greeks, demonstrating the tragedy associated with ancient notions of techne. Tabachnick then incorporates Heidigger's work elaborating on his ideas of techne with tragedy versus the permanent imposition of technology. Tabachnick then synthesizes Heidigger's ideas into his own opinions of how if we do venture into the vast world of limitless technology then we should proceed with caution.
