# Reading Response Paper 1
The three authors all agree that techne is the ancient greek term for arts and crafts. However, Cuomo cits a large variety of technes ranging from medicine, to arts, to various forms of craftsmanship. In general, techne is viewed as a form of productive knowledge, oriented around the physical creation of things. 

Technology however, was a term that originated in the seventeenth, which joined techne with the suffix ology (branch of learning). According to Marx, this term was originally used for the branch of mechatronics however the term expanded to become the "key-term" to "chart the changing character of contemporary society" (Marx 563). 

While the terms share a lot of overlap, there are definite distinctions between the two terms. The main outline within this paper will focus on the following: Relation to Nature, Phantom Objectivity and Tragedy.

Tabachnick uses Heidegger's analysis of techne and technology, who argues that there needs to be a shift from technology back to techne. He argues that techne and technology are entirely different when it comes to nature. Techne, works within the bounds of nature and alters the object but not the essence of the object. For example, a woodworker can create a chair from a tree, which shapes the nature without obscuring the essence of the tree from the chair. On the other hand, technology obscures the essence of the object and is not limited by the bounds of nature. Things considered technology obscure their origin and essence as its hard to tell how these things come into being whereas the products of techne are often clear in origin and process.

Marx uses George Lukacs notions of technology having "phantom objectivity", which refers to technology obscuring the human connection and contribution to the object. This is a result of the process of reification which is endowing a human activity with the characteristics of a thing. This process gives the power to the object rather than the social, economic, and political relations that brought this change. For example, a statement such as "the tractor transformed the agriculture industry" embues the object tractor with the power as opposed to the humans that brought this change. Whereas with techne, the product as always associated with the maker, it is the makers work and the makers expertise that make the product excellent. 

Tabachnick analyzes the works of sophocles to show that tragedy is associated with techne. For the greeks, great technical achievements were often met with great tragedies (from what the greeks described as the forces of nature or divine wraith), reminding them about their human limitations and to not be greedy with exceeding these human limits. Technology on the other hand is the complete opposite, seemingly a limitless progress and endless inovations. Techne was associated with inevitable tragic limitations whilst technology's limits are seemingly solved with more technology.

Thus, while the origin of the term technology comes from techne, there is are strong distinctions between the two terms for their relation to nature, phantom objectivity and relation to tragedies.
