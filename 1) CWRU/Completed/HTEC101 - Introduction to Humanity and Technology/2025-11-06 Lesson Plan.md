# Notes on Text
In short, Galison believes that Science is too diverse to be generalized to a single model. Galison poses these problems to make a shift towards core collaborative problems that drive research. 

Galison brings up 10 problems with the study of History and Philosophy of Science which are:

1. Context of Argument: Historical context and conditions that shape the acquisition of knowledge.
2. Purity and Fundamentality: Difference of Sciences branching out from pure (acquisition of knowledge for curiosity) and more applied sciences (science to solve a problem)
3. Technologies of Argumentation: how have there been advancements in the way scientific argumentations are formed? Craftsman's toolkit analogy
4. Making things: Bluring the line of artificial vs natural
5. What should we make: Ethical creations of creating certain things
6. Political Technologies: ie technologies made for the sack of politics, inherent vs consequential poltics of technology
7. Locality and Microhistory: Questions the issues / difficulties that come with looking at history on small scale
8. Globality: Issues that science is becomming increasingly more global and disregarding microhistory
9. Relentless / Escape History: Questions whether or not there exists a timeless scientific process or reasoning or if everything is merely a product of the current time
10. Scientific Doubt: How should we understand scientific doubt when it was constructed for political gain or commercialized weapons.

In short, how have these problems shaped our understanding of science (and its methods) as a whole?
