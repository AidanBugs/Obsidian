## 4 (10 points) 
What is the purpose of surveillance systems? Describe the components of a surveillance system. Discuss the limitations of data collected from surveillance systems.

> Figure 4.6 in the textbook gives a perfect diagram for the components of a surveillance system. It starts with the actual occurence of a health event and of course confirmation of the event. Next there are a wide variety of reporters such as healthcare providers, schools, hospitals etc, that report these cases to a wide variety of data recipients. The data recipients are either county, state or federal health department that are then in charge of the data management and can then give feedback to the reporting sources as well as spread information to the public to take action. 
>
> Overall the purpose of these systems is to identify trends early and mobilize rapid response mitigating the morbitity and mortality of diseases. 
>
> However a major flaw of these systems mainly come from either incorrect reports (ie providers failing to fill out the forms for all cases) or simply patients not seeking medical attention. The latter can be from a variety of reasons such as lack of access to healthcare, patient being asymptomatic, or even choosing not to go due to monetary restrictions. These reasons can cause a slight under reporting of the actual amount of cases compared to the reported amount.

Define the term ‘big data’. Using your own ideas, state which data sources might be included in big data. What do you believe are some of the advantages and disadvantages of using big data for epidemiologic research?

> First off big data is defined by the 3 v's:
>
> 1. Volume: large quantity of data
> 2. Velocity: high frequency of new data points
> 3. Variety: different types of data (structured, unstructured)
>
> Essentially, big data refers to large quantities of data that are rapidly updating in a wide variety of data formats. Examples of data sources could be social media posts, traffic data, weather data, etc. 
>
> For epidemiologic research there are pros and cons to using big data. For starters, the quality of the data is highly variablel based on the reputability of the sources as well as the overall completeness or representativeness of the data (is the data an accurate representation of the studied population or just a subset). Additionally, certain datasets are only it is important to use the data for the right use cases. Additionally, there are different ethical concerns when it comes to lack of informed consent and that correlation doesn't prove causation. Also, due to the nature of large data it may take some time to transform the data into a more usuable data type (example could be a JSON format), becaause often times big data is unstructured. However despite all of these downsides there are a lot of benefits of big data such as having a wide variety and large quantity of data ensuring that usually data trends are statistically significant (due to law of large numbers).

## 3 (10 points) 
Define the terms case reports an case series. Indicate how they are similar and how they differ. Search the internet for examples of case reports and case series of disease

> Case reports refer to a single report of noteable health incident while case series refer to a collection of reports grouped by common features (ie symptoms of affected patients). They are similar as they both are referring to a single disease / health event but they differ from the fact that case reports refer to the individual reports while case series reefers to a collection of the report. Another way of thinking about the difference is a case report is the descriptive report of a single patient while the case series refers to a collection of patients with the same disease.
>
> Example I found on the internet is "A rare case of recurrent pericarditis induced by iodinated contrast media" which is a case report of a single individual while "Outcomes of patients with recurrent preicarditis treated with Colchicine: A Case Series of 20 patients" refers to a case series of 20 patients and their health outcomes.
