# 1. Term
Automation

# 2. Definition
Automation is when there is technology used to perform tasks with minimal outside intervention. Automation can take place in a variety of forms through the use of machines, software or even physics to repeat or reproduce the task at hand. 

# 3. Citation
"Some Moral and Technical Consequences of Automation" by Norbert Wiener

# 4. Example
Contemporary examples of automation include a variety of different disciplines. The more common ones could be the machines used in factories to create an "assembly line" which allows pieces to be compiled sequentially through machines with minimal intervention from outside the assembly line. Other examples could be using AI to transcribe meetings instead of needing a scribe to write down the things said. In Norbert Wiener's text, he described using machines to play gamess itself so that way the machine could learn faster. Another way to make sense of Wiener's Time Scale problem is to imagine a factory machine that constantly produces a product in the programmed way. Working alongside a human QA agent, if the QA agent discovers an issue with the machine's outputted product, then there is a bigger problem beyond a single failed product but a whole batch of products. This is why when there are product recalls they recall a batch of products at a time because of the automated production already produced many deffective products.
