I read the article “Distinct AI Models Seem To Converge On How They Encode Reality,” by Quanta Magazine and found this to be extremely interesting. For starters they explained the concepts of embeddings extremely well for anyone to understand. In short, the paper explained how embeddings are AI's way of 'understanding' words and sentences by converting them to a long list of numbers in a vector space. Words that are similar (typically meaning they can be exchanged with eachother in a sentence) will be closer to eachother in this vector space. For example, words like good and great would be similar to eachother so they would be close to each other in the vector space. The paper explained how the larger more successful AI models have very similar embedding frameworks despite being completely different models. They tested  this by finding the similar words for any given input and saw that the larger models had similar similarity lists. The article then explored different papers and theories on this topic, where some researchers theorized if the LLM embeddings are converging then the AI could be grasping some crazy ideal numerical representation of the world as we know it, especially if the AI's are trained on a variety of different data sets and still coming to the same convergent features. This article made me wonder if we do hypothetically have a convergent embedding system across these high performant AI, does that mean that they are discovering an objective truth about numerical representation of reality or are they simply perfecting a representation of semantics?
